HARO shut down in December 2024. Then it came back. Then it got renamed again. If you've searched for "HARO link building" in 2026 and got confused, that's reasonable — the platform went through three brand names in eighteen months.

The short version: the tactic still works. Journalist queries remain one of the [highest-ROI link building channels](/blog/saas-backlink-building) available to a SaaS founder who can provide genuine subject-matter expertise. The platform landscape changed and the bar for a good pitch is higher, but nothing about the underlying mechanics has changed.

This guide covers the current 2026 platform landscape, what makes a pitch win, and how to build a repeatable system without hiring anyone.

## What Are Journalist Queries and Why They Work

Journalist queries are requests from journalists, editors, and bloggers who need expert sources for articles already in progress. You respond with a pitch positioning yourself as a credible source. If selected, you get quoted — and the article links back to your site.

The link quality is high for two reasons.

First, editorial placements live on domains that earned their authority through real publishing, not link schemes. A placement on a DR 70+ publication carries significantly more SEO value than most links you can buy at any reasonable price. According to [BuzzStream data](https://www.buzzstream.com/blog/link-building-statistics/), the average cost of a quality backlink purchased through paid means is $508–$750 per link. Journalist query placements earn comparable quality at zero cash cost.

Second, Google's quality assessments reward genuine experience and expertise. A journalist selecting you as a named source for an article they're writing is a strong E-E-A-T signal — you're being vouched for by an independent editorial process, not writing about yourself.

There's also a newer reason to care: AI search citations. Publications with real editorial standards are disproportionately cited in ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews responses. Every placement in a qualified publication increases the probability your brand surfaces when AI engines answer questions in your space. The connection between digital PR placements and AI citation visibility is real and growing — [BuzzStream's 2026 research](https://www.buzzstream.com/blog/digital-pr/) found 75% of digital PR professionals have already been asked about it by clients, but only 11% have a reliable process.

## The HARO Timeline: 2024–2026

If you've seen HARO described differently in different places, here's what actually happened:

- **2008:** Peter Shankman founds HARO (Help A Reporter Out)
- **2014:** Cision acquires HARO
- **2023:** Cision renames HARO to "Connectively" — community frustration follows
- **December 9, 2024:** Cision shuts Connectively down entirely
- **April 2025:** Featured.com (Brett Farmiloe, CEO) [acquires HARO's assets](https://www.prweek.com/article/1914439/featuredcom-acquires-help-reporter-cision) and relaunches the service free of charge
- **June 2025:** Featured.com rebrands the revived service back to "Connectively" under their ownership

In parallel, Peter Shankman — the original HARO founder — launched **Source of Sources (SOS)** in response to the shutdown, taking a more curated, invite-based approach.

What this means if you're starting in 2026:

- Your old HARO account doesn't carry over — create a new one on the current platform
- The revived Connectively launched free in April 2025; verify current pricing before signing up
- Journalist volume has rebuilt, but it's not at HARO's 2020–2022 peak yet

## Setting Up Your Expert Profile

Before pitching, every platform asks you to create a source profile. Here's what journalists actually see and use to filter:

- **Name and title** — be specific. "Founder at [Company]" is weaker than "CEO at [Company], 7 years in B2B SaaS"
- **Area of expertise** — this determines which query categories you receive
- **Bio** — 2–3 sentences maximum; lead with your credibility on the topic, not your company
- **Website URL** — where the link will point

**The single biggest mistake:** pitching outside your listed expertise. If your profile says B2B SaaS pricing and you respond to a query about e-commerce logistics, journalists filter you out immediately. Keep your profile matched to what you actually know, and set up category alerts so you only receive relevant queries. Responding to everything with a generic pitch is the fastest way to waste time and build a poor approval rate.

## How to Write a Pitch That Gets Selected

The average journalist receives [around 63 pitches per day](https://www.prca.org.uk/The-things-journalists-hate-most-about-PR-pitches) for each query. Most are too generic, too long, or don't directly answer the question being asked.

**Response rate benchmarks by pitch type:**

| Pitch type                                       | Approval rate |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ------------- |
| Generic template (name, title, vague offer)      | ~3%           |
| Personalized but no specific proof of experience | ~8–12%        |
| Data-backed or experience-specific               | ~18%          |
| Responded within 1 hour of query publication     | ~20%          |

Sources: [Linkorite benchmarks](https://linkorite.com/benchmarks/haro-response-success-rate); [VisibilityStack campaign data (143 placements from 1,000+ pitches)](https://www.visibilitystack.ai/academy/content-engineering/best-journalist-query-platforms).

![Two side-by-side pitch cards — left card has flat gray text and a red X marking it as generic, right card has a line highlighted in orange, a small bar chart icon, and a green checkmark marking it as data-backed, with a cursor hovering over the winning card](/resources/haro-link-building/pitch-quality.webp)

**The anatomy of a winning pitch:**

1. **Lead with the specific credential** that makes you the right source for _this_ query — not your company, your relevant experience
2. **Answer the journalist's question directly** — give them a usable quote or data point in the first 150 words
3. **Add one concrete proof element** — a number, a specific outcome, a personal finding that couldn't come from a general AI response
4. **Stay under 250 words** — longer pitches get skimmed or skipped entirely

Here's the difference between a pitch that gets deleted and one that gets used:

Hi [Name],

I run [Company], a B2B SaaS tool. We've run six pricing experiments over 18 months — two of which increased MRR by over 20% without changing the product.

[Name] | CEO, [Company] | [URL]

</EmailDraft>

The second pitch answers the question, leads with a specific number, and includes a finding that couldn't be produced without real experience. That's the signal journalists are looking for — and why data-backed pitches earn roughly 6× the placement rate of generic ones.

**What gets rejected immediately:**

- Pitches that open with a company description instead of the relevant expertise
- Vague offers to "help with any questions"
- Pitches longer than 300 words
- Content that reads as AI-generated — journalists are actively filtering this in 2026
- Responses that don't address the actual question in the query

## Reactive PR: Earning Links from Breaking News

Reactive PR means pitching journalists on stories in progress — without using a platform. When a significant event happens in your space, journalists covering it need expert sources fast.

The window is short: **4–24 hours**. After that, most stories are filed.

**How to monitor:**

- **Google Trends** — spot rising topics in your industry in real time
- **`#journorequest`** on X/Twitter — journalists post source requests directly; set up a keyword search to catch relevant ones
- **Google Alerts** for your core keywords and competitors

A reactive pitch follows the same structure as a platform pitch, but the hook is the current event: "I saw you're covering [X] — here's what we've seen from our side that might be useful for your story."

Reactive PR can produce links from DR 80+ publications within hours when timed right. It's harder to systematize than platform-based pitching, but the link quality often exceeds what journalist query platforms produce — the stories tend to have wider readership and more inbound links from the start.

## How Journalist Placements Build AI Search Visibility

This part is newer and worth understanding before you dismiss it as hype.

![A curved orange arrow flows from a small publication card with a DR badge on the left to a rounded AI chat bubble on the right, where a brand name is highlighted in orange as an AI citation, with small sparkle elements around the mention](/resources/haro-link-building/ai-citations.webp)

AI answer engines — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews — build their responses by synthesizing content indexed across the web. Editorial publications with real standards are over-represented in those citations compared to brand-owned content, press releases, or low-DR blogs.

When you're quoted as a source in a DR 70+ article about B2B pricing, two things happen. The article passes traditional link equity to your domain. But it also creates an indexed, third-party association between your name, your company, and that topic — in a source that AI engines consider credible.

The practical result: brands that earn editorial mentions in qualified publications show up more frequently when AI engines synthesize answers on those topics. Getting into the right publications now, before this dynamic is fully understood by most founders, is one of the more asymmetric opportunities in search in 2026.

## What to Track and When to Expect Results

**Metrics worth checking for each placement:**

- **Referring domain DR** — quality of the link
- **Dofollow status** — editorial links are usually dofollow; confirm manually for each placement
- **Referral traffic** — shows up in Google Analytics within days of publication; signals that the page is getting real readers
- **Ranking movement** — typically visible at 3–6 months into a consistent campaign

**Realistic timeline:** Industry data puts initial results for [85.2% of campaigns](https://subscribepr.com/digital-pr-statistics/) within 3–6 months. Don't expect week-one ranking changes from a single placement. The compounding effect of 8–15 quality editorial links over 6 months is where the value materializes.

**Simple tracking setup:** A spreadsheet with columns for query date, platform, publication URL, DR, dofollow status, referral clicks, and ranking position for 2–3 target keywords is enough to start. You don't need software. You need consistency.

For a broader benchmark of outreach reply rates by DR tier and site fit outside journalist platforms, see [Mentiohunt's link building outreach statistics](/link-building-outreach-statistics-2026).

## The Complement to Journalist Outreach

Journalist queries require active effort — you're pitching, one query at a time. There's a parallel channel that requires almost none: [unlinked brand mentions](/blog/how-to-find-backlink-opportunities#step-5-convert-unlinked-mentions).

Every time someone writes about your product, your company, or your founders without linking to you, you have a low-friction link opportunity. The site already trusts your brand enough to mention it. A short email asking them to add a link converts far more reliably than [cold outreach to a stranger](/blog/link-building-outreach-email).

[Mentiohunt](/blog/how-to-find-backlink-opportunities) monitors the web for those existing mentions and surfaces them as a daily queue — with the contact details and a ready-to-send outreach draft. Outreach is scheduled automatically; you monitor the queue and cancel anything that isn't a fit.

Between active journalist pitching and passive mention monitoring, most early-stage founders can build a consistent, high-quality link acquisition workflow without hiring anyone or paying per link.
