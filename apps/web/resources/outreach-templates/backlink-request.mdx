Most backlink request emails get one read and a delete, not because the writing is bad, but because the ask arrives before any reason to say yes. The fix isn't a better-written template — it's sending the right template for what you actually found on their site, and skipping the ask entirely when the fit isn't there yet.

## Which backlink request actually fits your situation

Before picking a template, figure out what you're actually looking at. Each situation below calls for a different opening and a different ask.

| What you found                                                | Template to use          | The ask                                |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| They already named you, your product, or your data — unlinked | Unlinked mention request | Add a link to an existing mention      |
| You have a specific page that fits a specific spot on theirs  | Direct backlink request  | Add a link to your page                |
| No existing mention, but real audience overlap                | Partnership request      | No hard ask — open the relationship    |
| You sent one of the above and heard nothing                   | Follow-up                | Add something new, don't just check in |

The unlinked mention request is the one most template roundups bury near the bottom of a list of fifteen or twenty. It deserves to go first — you're not asking a stranger for anything, you're asking someone who already referenced you to finish the job.

## The four emails

### Unlinked mention

Use this when someone already named your product, cited your data, or referenced your content — without linking it.

<EmailDraft
  subject="Quick fix for your CRM roundup"
  to="Dana Ruiz"
  from="Tom Walsh, Capsule"
>

Hi Dana,

Saw Capsule mentioned in your roundup of CRM tools for small sales teams — appreciate the shoutout.

Noticed it isn't linked yet. Would you mind pointing it to capsule.io? Happy to send over anything else that'd be useful for the post.

Tom

</EmailDraft>

Don't send this if the mention is buried in a comment section or a low-traffic page you found by accident — confirm it's on a page worth having a link from before you ask.

The gap is the editorial decision. A site that already named you has decided you're worth referencing — you're asking for a link, not a mention.

### Direct backlink request

Use this when you have a genuinely useful page and a specific, visible reason it belongs on theirs — not a general "we'd love to be featured" pitch.

<EmailDraft
  subject="Addition for your email deliverability guide"
  to="Priya Nair"
  from="Tom Walsh, Capsule"
>

Hi Priya,

Read your guide on email domain warm-up — the section on daily sending limits was a useful breakdown.

One gap: setting up a separate sending subdomain so outreach volume doesn't affect a team's main product email reputation. We wrote a guide that covers exactly that: capsule.io/blog/email-deliverability

Would it be worth adding as a resource in that section?

Tom

</EmailDraft>

Don't send this if you can't point to the exact section, paragraph, or gap your page fills. "We think your readers would like our resource" without naming where it fits is the version that gets deleted.

### Partnership opener

Use this when a site covers your space and has [real audience overlap](/free-tools/competitor-backlink-gap), but hasn't mentioned you and there's no natural gap to point to yet.

<EmailDraft
  subject="Loved your post on outreach systems"
  to="Marcus Webb"
  from="Tom Walsh, Capsule"
>

Hi Marcus,

I run Capsule — a lightweight CRM for small B2B sales teams. Came across your post on outreach systems for early-stage teams and wanted to say it's one of the better breakdowns I've seen on keeping volume manageable without burning through prospects.

We've been working through a related problem on the deliverability side. Wrote up what we learned here, in case it's useful: capsule.io/blog/email-deliverability

No ask here — just wanted to flag it in case it's relevant to what you cover.

Tom

</EmailDraft>

Skip the ask entirely. This email exists to make a future request land better, not to get a link today.

### Follow-up

Send this four to six days after any of the above, if you haven't heard back.

<EmailDraft
  subject="Re: Addition for your email deliverability guide"
  to="Priya Nair"
  from="Tom Walsh, Capsule"
>

Hi Priya,

Following up on my note from a few days ago.

One thing I should have mentioned: the guide also covers checking whether outreach emails land in inbox vs. spam before scaling sends — often the next question readers have after the warm-up section.

Happy to answer anything.

Tom

</EmailDraft>

Never write "just checking in" on its own — it tells the reader you have nothing new to add. Give them a reason to reconsider, not a reminder.

## What makes a backlink request read as spam

- **Leading with your domain metrics instead of their page.** Opening with your DR or traffic numbers signals a mass send before the reader gets to the actual pitch.
- **Asking before giving a reason.** If the first sentence is the ask, there's no context for why it's worth a yes.
- **No specific placement suggested.** "Consider linking to us somewhere" is harder to act on than naming the exact section or sentence.
- **Framing it as a trade.** "I'll link to you if you link to me" reads as a swap, not a recommendation — and [reciprocal exchanges done at scale](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) carry their own risk.

## Before you send: quick checklist

- Does the first line reference something specific — a mention, a section, a page — not their site in general?
- Is the ask one sentence, answerable with a yes or no?
- Would this email make sense only to this recipient, or could it go to anyone in the niche?
- If it's a follow-up, does it add something new instead of just checking in?

If any of those come back no, it's worth another pass before sending.
