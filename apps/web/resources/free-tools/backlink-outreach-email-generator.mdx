## Why the angle matters more than the wording

Every backlink outreach email is a variation on the same four situations: you want a guest post, you found a broken link, you want added to a resource list, or someone already mentioned you without linking. Picking the wrong angle for the situation is the most common reason outreach gets ignored — a guest post pitch sent to a site that does not accept contributors wastes a reply on both ends.

The generator above produces one email per angle so you can send whichever actually fits the site you are targeting, instead of adapting a single generic template to situations it was not written for.

## The four outreach angles

**Guest post pitch** — the highest-effort ask, so it needs the most context. Reference something the site already publishes and explain why your topic extends it. Sites that do not publish contributor content will ignore this angle regardless of how well it is written; check for a "write for us" page or past guest posts first. Pairs well with the [guest post sites finder](/free-tools/guest-post-sites-finder) for identifying sites that already accept contributors.

**Broken link fix** — the strongest angle because you are solving a problem the site owner already has, whether they know it or not. Verify the link is actually broken before sending; a false claim destroys credibility on the first email.

**Resource page ask** — works on pages explicitly curating links ("resources", "tools we recommend", "further reading"), not regular blog posts. The intent to add more links already exists on these pages, which is why they reply at a higher rate than cold guest post pitches.

**Unlinked mention** — the easiest ask you will ever make. The site already wrote about you; you are only asking for a small edit. This consistently gets the highest reply rate of the four angles because there is effectively no editorial decision left to make — just adding a link to content that already exists.

## Getting the email in front of the right person

A well-written outreach email sent to a generic contact form still underperforms one sent to the actual author or site owner.
