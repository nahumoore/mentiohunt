## How the Backlink Opportunity Finder works

The tool reads your website to extract your product category, use cases, and target audience. It then scans relevant websites, content hubs, and resource pages to find places where linking to your product would serve their readers.

Results fall into four opportunity types:

**Resource pages** — curated link lists in your niche that already link out to similar tools, guides, or resources. These are the highest-converting backlink target because the intent to link is built in.

**Link roundups** — recurring posts (weekly, monthly) that aggregate useful content in a category. Getting included requires submitting your content or tool for consideration before the next edition.

**Blog mentions** — individual posts that discuss a problem your product solves, where a reference to your tool would improve the article for the reader.

**Competitor blog posts** — posts that already reference a competing tool. If your product is a strong alternative, you have a clear angle for outreach.

Each result includes a plain-language fit rationale — why this site was surfaced — so you can prioritize without reading every page first.

## Three ways founders use this tool

**Building an initial outreach list.** When you're starting link building from scratch, you need a qualified starting point that isn't a generic "high DA sites" spreadsheet. Run your homepage URL and start with the resource page opportunities — they have the clearest path to a link.

**Finding specific page-level targets.** If you've published a new guide, tool, or data piece, enter that page's URL instead of your homepage. The tool will surface sites where that specific content fits — resource pages that cover the same topic, roundups in that category, related posts where a reference would add value.

## What the tool cannot tell you

Opportunity quality depends on whether the target site is actively maintained, accepts outreach, and has editorial standards worth meeting. The tool can surface fit — it can't verify whether the site owner responds to emails or whether a link from that domain has passed a manual penalty.

Always check a site manually before spending time on outreach: read a few recent posts, verify the contact method exists, and confirm the page linking structure makes sense. A fit rationale is the starting point for due diligence, not a replacement for it.
