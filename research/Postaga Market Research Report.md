# Postaga Market Research Report

## Executive Summary

Postaga is an AI‑powered outreach platform focused on automating link‑building, PR‑style content promotion, and sales prospecting by handling research, contact discovery, and email‑sequence execution in one interface.  It targets founders, SEOs, marketers, and small agencies who want to scale backlink campaigns without assembling a full stack of separate tools.  Customers tend to choose Postaga when they value an all‑in‑one workflow, strong AI‑driven campaign suggestions, and relatively low entry pricing, but they may trade off a more modern UI and richer CRM depth.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6][^7]

For Mentiohunt, the key overlap is that both products sit in the “outbound opportunity discovery and outreach” space, but Postaga leans into end‑to‑end campaign automation while Mentiohunt currently focuses on opportunity discovery, fit/rating, and community‑based reply suggestions.[^8][^9]

***

## Product Overview

### Core product category

Postaga positions itself as an AI‑assisted, all‑in‑one outreach and link‑building platform, combining prospect research, contact finding, and email‑sequence automation.[^4][^1][^8]

### Primary use cases

- Automated link‑building and guest‑post outreach campaigns.[^9][^4]
- PR‑style content promotion and partnership outreach.[^5][^8]
- Sales prospecting with personalized email sequences.[^10][^5]


### Main workflows

Postaga’s workflow typically starts with a URL or topic, then:

1. The AI analyzes the content and suggests campaign types (e.g., skyscraper, guest post, podcast outreach).[^11][^8]
2. The Opportunity Finder surfaces relevant websites or prospects.[^8][^11]
3. The Contact Finder pulls emails and social profiles.[^10][^8]
4. The platform builds and sends personalized email sequences with throttling and follow‑ups.[^4][^5]

### Key user personas

- Solo SEO practitioners and freelance link‑builders.[^7][^12]
- In‑house marketing teams at small‑to‑mid SMBs.[^6][^7]
- SEO and PR agencies running outreach at scale.[^5][^9]


### Company or product background

Postaga was built as a free, AI‑assisted outreach tool for link‑building and traffic growth, later expanding into paid tiers and agency plans.  It is marketed as a “better way to build links” by bundling research, contact discovery, and email campaigns.  Public profiles indicate Postaga LLC is headquartered in the U.S., with a focus on SEO and PR‑driven outreach.[^13][^14][^15][^4]

### Market positioning

Postaga positions itself as an affordable, AI‑powered “all‑in‑one outreach assistant” that competes with more polished but pricier tools like Respona or BuzzStream, while still being more workflow‑oriented than pure contact‑finding tools such as Hunter or SignalHire.[^11][^7][^10]

## Target Customers

### Company size

Postaga’s pricing and plan structure suggest it is best suited for:

- Micro‑teams and solos (Free/Pro).[^2][^3]
- Small‑to‑mid SEO agencies and in‑house teams (Agency plan).[^3][^16]
Capterra lists it as relevant for companies from self‑employed up through enterprises, but the pricing and feature set imply strongest fit in the 1–100‑person range.[^6]


### Roles or teams

- SEO specialists and link‑builders.[^9][^4]
- In‑house marketing and growth teams.[^7][^6]
- PR and media outreach teams.[^5][^8]
- Sales development and outbound prospecting teams.[^10][^5]


### Industries or verticals

Postaga is not tightly verticalized but is most commonly used in:

- SEO, content marketing, and digital marketing agencies.[^12][^5]
- SaaS and tech companies running content‑driven link‑building.[^17][^9]
- E‑commerce and DTC brands promoting content and PR.[^7][^5]


### Technical sophistication required

Postaga is designed to be low‑to‑mid technical, with:

- Pre‑built campaign types and AI‑assisted templates.[^8][^11]
- Amplified data via integrations (e.g., Ahrefs) handled through simple account connections.[^17]
Users need comfort with CSV‑style inputs and basic SMTP/email‑provider setup, but not full‑stack dev skills.[^17][^5]


### Fit by team type

- **Founders / small teams:** Good fit for those who want to run campaigns themselves without heavy dev overhead.[^12][^7]
- **In‑house marketers / SEOs:** Strong fit for ongoing link‑building and content promotion.[^4][^9]
- **Agencies:** Agency plan is explicitly framed for teams running multiple accounts.[^16][^3]
- **Sales teams:** Positionable for sales prospecting, but not its primary emphasis.[^5][^10]

It is not obviously positioned as an enterprise‑grade CRM or sales‑stack core, and public materials lean toward “small teams and agencies.”[^6][^7]

## Pricing

Postaga pricing is not fully disclosed in public, but recent analyses and directory listings converge on a clear structure.[^2][^3][^16]

### Free plan or trial availability

- A free plan exists, typically capped at a small number of campaigns and searches (e.g., 3 campaigns and 50 searches).[^2][^7]
- A 14‑day free trial is also offered for paid plans, allowing access to higher usage limits.[^3][^7]


### Paid plans and prices

Public summaries from 2026 give:


| Plan | Monthly list price | Annual price | Contacts | Emails | Daily limit | Seats / users | Source |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Pro | \$99 | \$84 | 2,000 | 10,000/month | 300/user/day | 5 | [^3][^16] |
| Agency | \$299 | \$250 | 15,000 | 100,000/month | 300/user/day | Unlimited | [^3][^16] |

Capterra lists starting price at \$99/month, with tiers including Free, Pro, and Agency.[^2][^6]

### Billing intervals

- Monthly and annual billing are available; annual discounts are explicit (e.g., Pro at \$84/month when billed annually).[^16][^3]


### Usage limits

- Contacts and emails are the main caps: Pro allows 2,000 contacts and 10,000 emails/month; Agency allows 15,000 contacts and 100,000 emails/month.[^3][^16]
- Searches are also limited (e.g., 1,000 searches/month on Pro, 15,000 on Agency).[^16][^7]


### Feature gates by plan

- Pro includes unlimited campaigns, SMTP integration, custom signatures, and campaign tools.[^16]
- Agency adds unlimited users, additional accounts, and enhanced support.[^7][^16]
- The free plan appears heavily gated for evaluation (small search and campaign caps), which reviewers note as a limitation.[^4][^7]


### Enterprise or custom pricing

- No explicit enterprise or “custom” pricing page is surfaced in public sources; reviewers instead describe the Agency plan as the top tier for agencies.[^3][^7]
- Reviews imply that special‑deal or AppSumo‑lifetime customers may receive different support or feature access, but this is not a visible public enterprise SKU.[^19][^7]


### Refunds, cancellation, or contract information

- Public sources do not document clear refund or cancellation policies; this information likely appears in terms of service or checkout flows not visible in directories.[^6][^3]
- No‑contract, month‑to‑month billing is implied by flat‑rate SaaS pricing, but explicit confirmation is not available in the indexed sources.[^2][^3]

## Customer Reviews And Sentiment

### Review scores

- Capterra: 4.6/5 from 67 reviews; 4.9/5 for customer service.[^7]
- G2: 4.5/5 from 10 reviews.[^7]
- AppSumo: ≈4.25–4.4/5 from ~38–40 verified users.[^19]


### Common praise themes

- Users praise the end‑to‑end workflow automation, especially for link‑building campaigns.[^12][^7]
- Many highlight the value proposition at around \$84/month annual for Pro, calling it “compelling” for solo SEOs and small agencies.[^3][^7]
- Reviewers note that the platform “just works” for basic outreach and campaign sequencing, after initial setup.[^19][^7]


### Common complaint themes

- The UI is frequently described as outdated, cluttered, or “unpolished,” especially compared with newer AI‑first tools.[^19][^7]
- Users complain that the free plan is too restrictive to judge the full value of the product.[^4][^7]
- Some AppSumo LTD‑deal holders report poor support responsiveness and SMTP‑related issues.[^19][^7]
