## Why a bad list costs more than a bad email

A weak subject line loses you one reply. A dirty list can cost you your sender reputation — and that affects every email you send after it, not just the ones on that list. Mailbox providers like Gmail and Outlook track bounce rates and spam complaints at the sending domain level. Push enough invalid addresses through and future emails to genuinely good contacts start landing in spam too, including ones you haven't sent yet.

For link building and partnership outreach specifically, there's a second cost: even addresses that technically accept mail can be dead ends. A `support@` alias monitored by three rotating people, or a `contact@mailinator.com` scraped from a stale directory listing, isn't going to result in the reply you're actually after — a founder or site owner deciding your content is worth linking to.

## What each check in the tool catches

Paste your list above and each address gets sorted into one of five categories:

- **Valid** — well-formed syntax, live domain with a working mail server. The address can receive mail.
- **Invalid syntax** — malformed from the start: missing `@`, no domain, stray characters. These never had a chance.
- **Disposable** — the domain belongs to a known temporary-inbox provider. Technically deliverable, practically useless for outreach that expects a person to read and reply later.
- **Role-based** — a generic inbox like `info@`, `support@`, or `hello@` rather than a named person. Lower reply rates for cold outreach; fine for other use cases.
- **Unknown** — the domain accepts mail but the specific mailbox couldn't be confirmed without an actual send attempt. Not a red flag on its own, just less certain than a full pass.

Each layer catches a different kind of dead end. Syntax and disposable checks remove addresses that were never going to work. Role-based flags don't mean "don't send" — they mean "expect a lower reply rate, and prioritize named contacts first if you have a limited number of sends."

## How to use it before an outreach push

Run your list through before you send, not after. If you're building a prospect list manually — pulling emails off author bios, contact pages, or a scraped directory — those are exactly the sources most likely to include a stale, disposable, or role-based address. Filtering upfront means the sends you actually make go to addresses with a real chance of reaching a person.
