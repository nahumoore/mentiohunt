## What Happened to Postaga?

Postaga appears to be unavailable. Its public homepage returned a 404 when checked in May 2026, and the product no longer looks accessible from the main domain.

That does not necessarily prove the company shut down. Domains move, products relaunch, and companies sometimes leave old pages broken without making a public announcement. But for a founder trying to choose a tool today, the practical answer is the same: do not build your outreach workflow around software you cannot reliably access.

If you were considering Postaga because an older article recommended it, use this page as a replacement guide. The better question is not "what was closest to Postaga?" It is "which tool replaces the part of Postaga I actually needed?"

## What Postaga Did Well

Postaga was strongest when you wanted to run outreach as a campaign. Its workflow typically started with a URL or topic, then helped suggest campaign types, find relevant websites, identify contacts, and send personalized email sequences.

That was useful if your team already knew it wanted to run link-building or PR outreach every week. Postaga bundled several parts of the outreach stack into one interface, which could reduce spreadsheet work and context switching.

The tradeoff is that you still need to operate the campaign. You choose the motion, review the prospects, manage sending setup, check the contacts, refine the copy, and decide how aggressively to follow up.

## Where Postaga Can Be Too Campaign-Heavy for Founders

Many founders do not start with the question "which outreach campaign should I launch?" They start with "where should I spend the next hour?"

That difference matters. Postaga gives you campaign templates, contact finding, email sending, follow-ups, and a lightweight pipeline. Those features are valuable when outreach is already a weekly operating motion. They can feel like too much when the founder is still trying to build a simple distribution habit.

There is also operational work behind an email automation workflow. SMTP setup, deliverability, contact review, list cleanup, campaign throttling, and follow-up rules all need judgment. AI can speed up the work, but it does not remove the need to decide whether an opportunity is actually worth pursuing.

Public review summaries also mention fit tradeoffs: some users on <a href="https://www.capterra.com/p/202725/Postaga/" rel="nofollow">Capterra</a> and <a href="https://appsumo.com/products/postaga/reviews/" rel="nofollow">AppSumo</a> describe Postaga's UI as dated or cluttered, the free plan as restrictive for evaluation, and the CRM/pipeline depth as lighter than dedicated sales tools. None of that makes Postaga a bad product. It means the tool is better suited to users who want outreach campaign machinery, not just a clearer queue of next actions.

## Prospecting: Campaign Templates vs Article-Level Fit

Postaga's prospecting is useful when you know the campaign type you want to run. Its AI-assisted workflow can suggest motions such as skyscraper outreach, guest posting, podcast outreach, or other content promotion campaigns from a URL or topic.

That is helpful for SEOs and marketers who already understand the playbook. They can pick a campaign type, collect prospects, filter the list, find contacts, and send sequences.

Mentiohunt's workflow is more content-led. You provide article URLs or a sitemap, and the system focuses on finding websites where each piece of content might be relevant. That is especially useful for founders who have published useful content but do not know how to turn it into practical outreach targets.

Neither approach guarantees a backlink. The practical advantage is avoiding irrelevant outreach. For founder-led distribution, a smaller queue with clearer rationale can be more useful than a larger campaign list that still needs heavy review.

## Outreach Prep and Sending

Postaga is stronger if you want email campaign automation. It combines contact discovery, AI-assisted personalization, SMTP sending, follow-ups, throttling, and campaign tracking. For teams running repeated outreach, those controls can save time.

Mentiohunt is intentionally lighter. It is not trying to be a full outbound email platform first. It focuses on preparing the opportunity: why this target fits, what angle makes sense, who the recipient might be, and what draft you can start from.

That distinction is important for founders. Sending more emails is not always the bottleneck. Often, the bottleneck is knowing which opportunities are worth pursuing and why. Mentiohunt is designed to reduce that decision friction before outreach happens.

Review every AI draft before sending. Check the recipient, verify the page context, make sure the pitch is accurate, and remove anything generic. Link outreach works best when it is specific, useful, and easy for the recipient to evaluate.
