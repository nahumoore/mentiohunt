## What a press release actually needs to include

A press release is a factual document, not a marketing pitch — the format exists so a journalist can lift details directly into a story without calling you to verify basics. Every standard release includes six parts: a headline, a dateline, a lead paragraph, a quote, a company boilerplate, and a media contact block.

Skipping any of these doesn't make the release more casual — it makes it look unfinished to anyone who reads press releases regularly.

## The four announcement types

**Product launch** — lead with what the product does and who it's for, not how it was built. "A new tool that does X for Y" beats a paragraph about the team's background in the first sentence.

**Funding round** — state the amount, the round stage, and the lead investor in the lead paragraph. Save the "what we'll use it for" detail for the second sentence or the quote — it's the part reporters usually ask about anyway.

**Partnership** — name both companies and the concrete thing customers get from the partnership, not just that a deal was signed. "Company A integrates with Company B so customers can do X" is a lead; "Company A and Company B are excited to announce a partnership" is not.

**Milestone / traction** — pick a specific, verifiable number (customers, revenue, usage) rather than a vague superlative. "10,000 teams" is quotable. "Explosive growth" is not.

## Getting it in front of someone who'll actually read it
