Most blogger outreach emails fail for the same reason: the sender picked one template and used it for every situation, whether they were pitching a guest post, asking for a broken link fix, or just saying hello. Bloggers can tell. A guest post pitch and a "hey, loved this" note are not the same email, and using one for the other reads as a mismatch before the recipient even gets to your ask.

The fix is matching the template to what you actually found on the site, not defaulting to whichever one you remember.

## Pick the template that matches what you found

Before writing anything, figure out which situation you're actually in. Each one calls for a different opening, a different ask, and a different tone.

| What you found on their site                        | Template to use               | The ask                                  |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| They publish guest contributions                    | Guest post pitch              | Pitch one specific idea                  |
| They have a curated resources or tools page         | Resource page mention         | Suggest an addition                      |
| A post links to something broken or outdated        | Broken link replacement       | Offer your page as the fix               |
| They wrote about your space but never mentioned you | Collaboration / first contact | No ask — just get on their radar         |
| You sent one of the above and heard nothing         | Follow-up                     | Add something new, don't just "check in" |

Sending the wrong one out of habit is the most common way to waste a good prospect. A site owner with a resource page rarely wants a guest post pitch, and a founder who already [mentioned your product unlinked](/free-tools/backlink-outreach-email-generator) doesn't need to be introduced to who you are.

## The five templates

Each of these assumes you've already read the specific page you're referencing — not skimmed the homepage. The reference in the first line is what separates a real email from a template with a name swapped in.

### Guest post pitch

Use this when the blog publishes contributed posts and you have one specific idea, not a general "can I write for you" ask.

<EmailDraft
  subject="Guest post idea: email deliverability for early-stage SaaS founders"
  to="Marcus Webb"
  from="Tom Walsh, Capsule"
>

Hi Marcus,

I run Capsule — we build a lightweight CRM for small B2B sales teams. I've been reading GrowthHackers for a while; your post on outreach systems for early-stage teams was one of the better takes I've seen on keeping volume manageable without burning prospects.

I'd like to write a guest post on email deliverability setup for SaaS founders. My angle: most early-stage teams don't realize their cold outreach domain is affecting their product email reputation until it's already a problem — and the fix takes less than an hour.

Would that work for you?

Tom

</EmailDraft>

Pitch one idea, not a menu of three. A specific angle signals you've thought about their audience, not just their traffic.

### Broken link replacement

Use this when you find a dead or outdated link inside an existing post and have a page that genuinely fills the same gap.

<EmailDraft
  subject="Small addition for your email warm-up guide"
  to="Priya Nair"
  from="Tom Walsh, Capsule"
>

Hi Priya,

Read your post on email domain warm-up — specifically the section on daily sending limits. Useful breakdown.

One thing readers probably ask after that section: how to set up a separate sending subdomain so your outreach reputation doesn't affect your main domain. We wrote a guide that covers exactly that: capsule.io/blog/email-deliverability

If it fits what you'd want to point readers toward, happy to share more context.

Tom

</EmailDraft>

Name the exact section and the exact gap. "I noticed a broken link" without saying which one, or why your page is the right replacement, gets deleted.

### Collaboration / first contact

Use this when a blogger writes in your space but hasn't mentioned you yet, and you don't have a specific ask — just want to open a relationship.

<EmailDraft
  subject="Loved your post on outreach systems"
  to="Marcus Webb"
  from="Tom Walsh, Capsule"
>

Hi Marcus,

I run Capsule — we build a lightweight CRM for small B2B sales teams. I came across your post on outreach systems for early-stage teams on GrowthHackers and wanted to say it's one of the better breakdowns I've seen on keeping outreach volume manageable without burning through prospects.

We've been working through a related problem on the deliverability side — mainly how founders set up a sending domain without realizing it affects their main product email reputation. Wrote up what we learned here, in case it's useful: capsule.io/blog/email-deliverability

No ask here, just wanted to say hi and flag it in case it's relevant to what you cover.

Tom

</EmailDraft>

Skip the ask entirely. The goal is recognition, not a placement — this is the email that makes a future pitch land better because your name is already familiar.

This one isn't measured on replies — judge it on opens.

### Follow-up

Use this four to six days after any of the above, if you haven't heard back.

<EmailDraft
  subject="Re: Small addition for your email warm-up guide"
  to="Priya Nair"
  from="Tom Walsh, Capsule"
>

Hi Priya,

Following up on my note from a few days ago.

One thing I should have mentioned: the guide also covers how to check whether emails are landing in inbox vs. spam before scaling sends — something a lot of readers ask about in that context. Worth flagging in case it changes how it fits.

Happy to answer any questions.

Tom

</EmailDraft>

Never write "just checking in" — it signals you have nothing new to add. Give the reader a reason to reconsider, not just a reminder.

## What to personalize in every template

The templates above are a starting structure, not copy to paste as-is. Three things separate a personalized email from a template with a name swapped in:

- **The opening line** needs proof you read something specific — a claim, an example, a section, not "I loved your blog."
- **The value proposition** should describe [what their readers get, not what your company does](https://www.reddit.com/r/freelance/comments/ytj3z1/general_template_for_outreach_emails/). "This might help readers who want to go deeper on X" reads differently than "check out our tool."
- **The ask** should be one sentence, one question, and easy to answer with a yes or no. Multi-part asks force the reader to make several decisions at once, which is the easiest way to get no reply at all.

## Common mistakes that kill reply rates

- **Using the wrong template for the situation.** A guest post pitch sent to someone with no guest post history, or a broken-link email that doesn't name the actual broken link, both read as mass-sent regardless of how well-written they are.
- **Leftover template artifacts.** Brackets, placeholder names, or a subject line that doesn't match the body are [the fastest way to get filtered as spam](https://ahrefs.com/blog/link-outreach/).
- **Multiple asks in one email.** Pick one: a guest post, a mention, a link fix. Stacking asks makes the email harder to say yes to.
- **No reason for why you picked them specifically.** If the email could have been sent to any blog in the niche, it will get treated like it was.

## Before you send: a short checklist

- Does the first line reference something specific from their site, not their site in general?
- Is there exactly one ask, phrased as a yes/no question?
- Would this email make sense only to this recipient, or could it go to anyone?
- Did you check for leftover brackets, placeholder names, or a mismatched subject line?

If you can't answer all four confidently, it's worth another pass before it goes out.
