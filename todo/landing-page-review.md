# Landing page (/) missing items using a Reddit search based on peoples questions and pain points.

---

1. No time-to-value signal — HIGH IMPACT

Reddit pattern: Every "should I try link building?" thread has someone asking "how long until I see results?" or "how
long until my first link?"

What's on the page: Nothing. The stats show "1,200+ opportunities surfaced/month" and "25+ backlinks acquired/month" —
but those are aggregate platform stats, not per-customer expectations.

What to add: A concrete anchor, even conservative:

- A FAQ answer: "Most customers get their first placed backlink within X days of setup."
- Or a timeline step in the How it Works flow: Setup → Queue → First placement (avg. X days)

Maya's testimonial mentions "15 minutes a week" — that's time cost, not time-to-value. Both matter and neither is
prominent.

---

2. No visualization of what you actually approve — HIGH IMPACT

Reddit pattern: People don't trust what they can't see. The #1 complaint about agencies is opacity. The comparison table
says "Per-opportunity approval ✓" but there's no screenshot of what that looks like.

What's on the page: The "AI Citation Engine" section shows a fake article card (quorage.com, saasweekly.io) — these look
like opportunity examples but are illustrative, not real.

What to add: A real or high-fidelity mockup of an opportunity card as it appears in the dashboard. What information does
the user see? Site name, DR, monthly traffic, fit rationale, outreach angle, email draft. This is the product. Show it.

---

3. "I've been burned by agencies before" is addressed softly — HIGH IMPACT

Reddit pattern: Multiple high-vote posts specifically about agency scams — someone paying $1,600/month for 2 years with
nothing to show, another "scammed by 2 agencies and one freelancer." This is a dominant conversation. The burned-buyer is
your most likely ICP.

What's on the page: Sarah Mitchell's testimonial ("We dropped our link building agency because we had no idea what we
were paying for") comes closest but it's in a carousel. The Zero Agency Markup section is about price transparency, not
trust recovery.

What to add: A dedicated section or a bolded paragraph that speaks directly to this fear. Something like: "If you've been
burned by an agency, here's what's different — you see the exact site, the fit reason, and the outreach draft before we
send a single email. Nothing moves without your approval." Own the objection before it kills the conversion.

---

4. Guest posting is never mentioned — HIGH IMPACT

Reddit pattern: The most active link building discussion on Reddit right now is guest posting. "Is guest posting still
working in 2025?" was posted with discussion. It's the first tactic people think of when they think link building.

What's on the page: "Editorial placement," "mentions," "AI Citation Engine" — but never the word "guest post." Someone
who's been researching link building on Reddit will land on your page wondering: Do they do guest posts? Product
roundups? HARO mentions? All of the above?

What to add: Either explicitly include guest posting in the "What we do" description, or add a FAQ that answers: "Do you
do guest posting?" — with a clear yes/no and what type of placements you target.

---

5. "Does link building still work post-HCU / in the AI era?" — MEDIUM-HIGH IMPACT

Reddit pattern: The most-referenced SEO event is Google's Helpful Content Update (HCU). The expert consensus in the
community is that editorial links are more important after HCU, not less. But this isn't obvious to founders who've seen
generic SEO content get devalued.

What's on the page: The AI Visibility section does the AI angle well (mentions → AI answers). But the traditional SEO
angle ("links still rank you on Google, and post-HCU it matters more than ever") isn't addressed. A skeptic thinking "is
SEO even worth it anymore?" has no answer here.

What to add: One sentence in the hero or the AI section: "Links remain Google's #1 ranking signal. Post-HCU, it's the
low-quality content that got devalued — not the links pointing to good content." Short validation, not a blog post.

---

6. No real placement proof — MEDIUM IMPACT

Reddit pattern: Community members consistently ask for real examples: "Show me an actual link you placed — the URL, the
article, the anchor." The "100+ founders" and the GSC screenshot are useful but not specific enough.

What's on the page: Logan Stuart's testimonial is the only named real customer. The GSC screenshot shows growth but
doesn't connect it to specific placed links. The "quorage.com" and "saasweekly.io" examples look illustrative.

What to add: One real case — even if it's Mentiohunt itself. Site name, DR, article title, anchor text, date placed.
"Here's a link we placed for [customer] in [month]: [URL] — DR 58, 40k/month traffic, in an article about [topic]." One
concrete example beats eight abstract testimonials.

---

7. "Is this AI-generated spam?" — MEDIUM IMPACT

Reddit pattern: Emerging fear in 2025: AI-generated outreach emails that get immediately detected and trashed. Someone
returned to SEO after 7 years asking "how is this done now with ChatGPT?" — it's a real question. Site owners are getting
increasingly suspicious of AI-written pitches.

What's on the page: The "Always learning agent" section mentions monitoring Reddit/X/LinkedIn which sounds fully
automated. The "suggested outreach angles" feature in pricing implies the emails aren't final. But there's no reassurance
that outreach isn't AI template spam.

What to add: A line clarifying the human-in-the-loop nature of the emails. Even if it's just "Outreach is personalized
per site, not mass-templated" — it matters to the informed buyer.

9. "25+ backlinks acquired every month" needs context — LOW-MEDIUM IMPACT

Is that per customer? In total across the platform? For the top tier plan? A founder on the $49 Pro plan reading "25+
backlinks/month" may think that's their expected output. If it's actually 2–3 placements/month for a single site, set
expectations correctly or it becomes a churn trigger.
