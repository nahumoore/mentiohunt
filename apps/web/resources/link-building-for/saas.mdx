## Why SaaS link building isn't like other industries

SaaS has one real advantage most industries don't: a naturally citable content format. Original data, comparison pages, free tools, and opinionated frameworks all fit the way SaaS content already gets written — which means link building for SaaS is often more about surfacing what already deserves a link than inventing new outreach angles from scratch.

The tradeoff: the SaaS space is crowded with other SaaS companies doing the exact same content-led link building, so a generic "10 tips" post won't earn links the way it might have five years ago. What still works is specific.

## Tactics ranked by effort vs. payoff

### 1. Unlinked brand mentions (lowest effort, fastest turnaround)

If your product has been mentioned in an article, roundup, or comparison without a link, that's the easiest ask in link building — the writer already decided you were worth including. A short, polite note asking them to add the link converts at a far higher rate than any cold pitch.

### 2. Journalist and expert-source query platforms

Tech and business reporters constantly need sources to comment on industry trends, pricing changes, or product categories. A founder or team member responding quickly with a specific, quotable take earns editorial links that would take weeks of cold outreach to replicate.

### 3. Competitor backlink gap analysis

Find the sites linking to two or three direct competitors but not to you. These sites have already shown they're willing to cover something in your exact category — the fit work is done before you send a single email. A [free backlink opportunity finder](/free-tools/backlink-opportunity-finder) or [competitor gap tool](/free-tools/competitor-backlink-gap) can surface this list from a URL in a couple of minutes instead of a manual crawl.

### 4. Broken link building

Find dead links on pages relevant to your category — a "best tools for X" roundup, a resource page, a competitor that shut down — using a [broken link checker](/free-tools/broken-link-finder), then suggest your page as the replacement. It's a genuine win for the site owner (a fixed link) as much as it is for you, which makes the outreach easier than a cold ask. The [broken link building template](/outreach-templates/broken-link-building) covers how to pitch it.

### 5. Original data and benchmark pages

A page built around real numbers — usage benchmarks, pricing trends, a survey of your own customer base — gets cited by other writers covering the same topic because it does their research for them. This is the highest-payoff tactic on this list, and also the slowest to produce.

### 6. Guest posts and contributed articles

Still effective, but only on sites that actually publish for your audience — SaaS, startup, and niche B2B blogs your buyer already reads, not generic marketing blogs accepting any pitch. The [guest post outreach template](/outreach-templates/guest-post) covers how to pitch this without sounding like the hundred other guest post emails already in that editor's inbox.

### 7. Integration and partner co-marketing

If you integrate with another tool, a joint blog post, comparison page, or "better together" landing page usually earns a natural link from the partner's site — and it reaches an audience that's already a good fit.

## Who's actually doing this well

Zapier and HubSpot get cited constantly as SaaS link building examples for the same reason: neither relies on outreach volume. Zapier's integration directory pages and HubSpot's free tools and original research keep getting referenced by other writers covering the same topics, because the pages are useful enough to link to on their own. Neither company invented a new tactic — they just built enough genuinely citable pages that the tactics above started working with less manual effort behind each one. That's the target, not the exception.

---

## What to skip

- **Directory link building as a primary strategy.** G2, Capterra, and Product Hunt listings are worth claiming for traffic and social proof, but most are nofollow. Don't count them toward your core link building goal.
- **Paid guest post networks.** The same networks that pitch law firms and ecommerce brands also blanket SaaS founders' inboxes. Low relevance, real risk, no real payoff.
- **Broad "resource page" link building with no product connection.** Getting listed on a generic "startup tools" page with 200 other links does very little. Prioritize pages narrow enough that your listing is genuinely useful to the reader.
